An ongoing effect, such as being on fire, keeps damaging you. It is written "X persistent [type] damage", where X is the damage and [type] its type.

- It can be doubled or halved by an attack roll or saving throw, like normal damage.
- You take it at the end of each of your turns while you have the condition, rolling any dice again each time.
- After taking it, roll a DC 15 flat check. On a success, the condition ends.
- It ends on its own when it has run its course; the GM decides when, usually after 1 minute.

**Assisted recovery.** You or an ally can take steps that would reasonably help, usually a 2-action activity such as smothering flames or washing off acid. You then attempt an extra flat check at once, at most once per round. Depending on the help, the GM may require a check, lower the flat check DC to 10, end the condition outright (healing to full Hit Points ends bleed; jumping in a lake ends fire), or change the actions needed.

**Immunities, resistances and weaknesses** apply to persistent damage, separately from any initial damage. If an effect negates the initial damage, it usually negates the persistent damage too.

**More than one.** You can have several persistent damage conditions of different types. For the same type, the higher amount overrides the lower; when it isn't clear which is higher, the GM chooses. All persistent damage happens at once, so it triggers things that react to damage only once (for example, it raises [[Dying|main:dying]] only once).

*Source: Player Core, p. 445.*
