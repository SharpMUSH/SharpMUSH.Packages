A **basic saving throw** uses the normal check procedure, with a standard set of damage outcomes. The target rolls the save against the effect's DC; “basic” describes the consequences, not a different die roll.

## Resolve a basic save

1. Read the spell or ability to find the save type, DC, damage and any additional effects. For a Wizard spell, the target normally saves against the Wizard's spell DC.
2. The target rolls a d20 and adds its modifier for that save, including applicable bonuses and penalties.
3. Use [[Degrees of Success|main:degrees_of_success]] to determine the target's result, including the critical boundaries and natural 20 or 1 adjustments.
4. Apply the corresponding damage outcome, then the effect's additional instructions and any applicable immunities, weaknesses or resistances.

| Target's save result | Damage from the effect |
|---|---|
| Critical success | None |
| Success | Half |
| Failure | Full |
| Critical failure | Double |

When halving damage, round down; halving 1 damage still deals a minimum of 1 damage. Some effects add conditions or other consequences to particular results while still using a basic save: read the entire effect.

## Electric Arc example

Assume the [[Wizard Learning Guide|main:wizard_learning_guide]] caster has spell DC17 and [[Electric Arc|main:electric_arc]] prepared. Spend two actions to cast it at one visible creature 20 feet away. At level 1, it deals 2d4 electricity damage with a basic Reflex save. Its range is 30 feet, and it permits one or two creature targets; this example chooses only one.

Suppose the target has Reflex +7 and rolls a 10: 10 + 7 = 17, meeting DC17 for a **success**. Suppose the damage dice are 2 and 3: 5 damage halved and rounded down is **2 electricity damage**. Intelligence sets this Wizard's DC; it is not added to this spell's damage.

For the same listed damage of 5, the four possible save outcomes would deal 0, 2, 5 or 10 electricity damage respectively, before any applicable damage defenses. These are the **target's** degrees of success. The caster does not make a spell attack roll for Electric Arc.

Electric Arc is a cantrip, so casting it leaves it prepared and spends no spell slot. One action remains on this assumed three-action turn. Follow [[Spell Preparation|main:spell_preparation]] to distinguish cantrips, prepared slots and focus spells, and [[Cast a Spell|main:cast_a_spell]] for the casting procedure.
