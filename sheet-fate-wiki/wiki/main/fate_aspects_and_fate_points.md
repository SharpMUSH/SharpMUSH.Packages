An aspect is a phrase that describes something true and important: *Ex-con turned locksmith*, *Owes the wrong people*, *The warehouse is on fire*. Aspects are always true, and they matter most when you use them.

## Kinds of aspect

- **Character aspects** are on your sheet: a **high concept** that sums up who you are, a **trouble** that keeps complicating your life, and others. `+sheet/aspect <slot>=<text>` writes one.
- **Situation aspects** describe a scene or a thing in it. They last as long as the situation does.
- **Boosts** are fleeting: they go away once they are used.
- **Consequences** are aspects for lasting harm: see [[Stress and Consequences|main:fate_stress_and_consequences]].

## Invoking

When an aspect would help you, invoke it: spend a fate point (or a free invocation) and either take +2 on your roll or reroll all four dice. Say why the aspect helps. On this game, `+sheet/spend Invoking <aspect>` spends the point where the room sees it, and `+check <skill>+2` adds the bonus.

## Compels

When an aspect would make life harder, the GM, or you, can compel it: the aspect brings a complication or a bad decision, and you get a fate point for accepting. You can refuse a compel by paying a fate point instead.

## Fate points and refresh

Your **refresh** is how many fate points you start a session with. At the start of a session, if you have fewer than your refresh, staff bring you up to it; if you have more, you keep them. `+sheet` shows both. Staff give points with `+sheet/award` and refresh you with `+sheet/refresh`.
