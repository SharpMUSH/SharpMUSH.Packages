Superheroes in Fate are ordinary Fate characters with a few more tools: an **origin story**, **super skills**, and powers that **bid fate points** against each other.

## Origin story

A superhero may write an origin story in place of a trouble: one aspect that says how they got their powers and why those powers cause them problems. *Dimensional Traveler* explains the powers and leaves the hero a fish out of water.

## Super skills

A super skill is a skill, or an approach, that is also a power. It costs 1 refresh, as a stunt does: `+super/add <skill>`.

- Used as an ordinary skill, it rolls as one.
- For a fate point, it doubles for one roll: `+super/use physique` on Superb (+5) Physique rolls at +10.
- After each use it steps back by one, until the next scene (`+super/refresh`).
- Stepped back to nothing, it is **exhausted**. The hero has to rest or recharge, and the GM brings it back with `+super/recharge`.

Maxine, a Valkyrie with Superb (+5) Physique, spends a fate point to throw a car at a supervillain. Then her Physique is Great (+4). If she keeps going until it is exhausted, she has to journey to Valhalla to drink from the Horn of Might.

## Powers against powers

A power that is not opposed works for a fate point, with no roll: `+super/bid <what it does>`, then `+super/end`. When another hero's power stands in the way, the two bid back and forth:

- `+super/cancel` bids 1 to stop the last power.
- `+super/raise <power>` bids 2 to stop it and use your own instead.
- `+super/yield` gives up.

If a cancel is left standing, it is a tie: nothing happens, and the points go to the GM. Otherwise the last power works, and whoever yielded takes every point bid. A long bid can swing the fate point economy a long way.

## Villains

The GM can mark NPCs as minions, villains and supervillains. A crowd of thugs is often one roll, or one use of a power. Villains pick up a power or two, and supervillains are full characters with an origin story of their own.
